Analysis of the Voynich Manuscript images reveals several potential indicators of chapter or section beginnings. These markers, while not consistently applied, offer clues to the manuscript's structure.

1.  **Illustration Placement:** The placement of large, elaborate illustrations frequently coincides with the start of what appears to be a new topic or section. For example, the large rosette diagrams in the biological section and the cosmological illustrations are often followed by significant textual passages. The frequency of these illustration-marked sections is approximately once every 5-10 folios within their respective sections.

2.  **Handwriting Style Variations:** Subtle shifts in handwriting style are observable at certain points in the manuscript. While the primary script remains consistent, variations in letter formation, slant, and line thickness occasionally occur, particularly at the beginnings of paragraphs following illustrations. This suggests a possible change in scribe or a deliberate stylistic choice to denote a new section. Frequency: Irregular, but noticeable in the pharmaceutical section.

3.  **Paragraph Indentation and Spacing:** Changes in paragraph indentation and vertical spacing sometimes mark potential section breaks. In several instances, a new paragraph begins with a larger indentation or increased spacing above the line, particularly following illustrations or diagrams. This is more evident in the herbal and astronomical sections. Frequency: Roughly once every 3-7 folios.

4.  **Marginalia and Unique Symbols:** While rare, the occurrence of marginalia and unique symbols (e.g., small drawings or unusual punctuation marks) near the beginning of certain text blocks might indicate new sections. These symbols appear infrequently and are not uniformly distributed throughout the manuscript. Frequency: Very low, approximately once every 20-30 folios.

5.  **Change in Ink Color/Quality:** Although subtle, there are instances where the ink color or quality seems to change slightly at the start of a new page or section. This may be indicative of the beginning of a new work session by the scribe. Frequency: Infrequent and difficult to determine without spectroscopic analysis.

It's important to note that these markers are not consistently present and may reflect the habits of different scribes or varying levels of attention to detail. Further analysis, including statistical analysis of text features and detailed comparison of illustration styles, is necessary to confirm these observations and identify more definitive chapter markers.
