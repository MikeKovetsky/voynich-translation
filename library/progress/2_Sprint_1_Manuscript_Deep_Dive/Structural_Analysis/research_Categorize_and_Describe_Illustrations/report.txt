The Voynich Manuscript contains numerous illustrations, which can be broadly categorized as follows:

1.  **Botanical Illustrations:** These are the most prevalent, depicting plants that are largely unidentifiable. Style: Often feature detailed root systems, leaves, and flower structures, but lack realistic proportions or identifiable characteristics. Content: Images of plants with fantastical shapes, colors, and arrangements of leaves and flowers. Placement: Predominantly on folios 1r-66v. Recurring Motifs: Paired plants, dissected roots, and unusual flower shapes. These do not appear to match any known terrestrial species.

2.  **Astronomical/Astrological Illustrations:** Found throughout the manuscript, especially within the circular diagrams. Style: Circular diagrams with labeled sections, often featuring human figures, stars, and zodiacal symbols. Content: Zodiac symbols, constellations, and celestial arrangements. Placement: Prominently on folios 67r-73r. Recurring Motifs: Zodiac symbols (e.g., Taurus, Gemini, Cancer), stars, and nude female figures holding stars, often arranged in circular formations.

3.  **Balneological/Pharmaceutical Illustrations:** Depicting interconnected tubs, pipes, and anatomical figures bathing in green liquid. Style: Intricate drawings showing complex networks of tubs and plumbing-like structures, alongside nude figures. Content: Bathing scenes, anatomical drawings, and possible medicinal preparations. Placement: Folios 75r-86v. Recurring Motifs: Nude female figures, green liquid, interconnected tubs and pipes, and anatomical details.

4.  **Cosmological Illustrations:** Large, fold-out diagrams with circular arrangements and labeled sections. Style: Large, complex diagrams with circular structures. Content: Potentially represent cosmological models, with sections labeled in the unknown script. Placement: Folios 86v. Recurring Motifs: Concentric circles, labeled sections, and fortress-like structures.

5.  **Recipe-like Pages:** Pages that combine text with small images of pharmaceutical jars and herbs. Style: Text-heavy pages with small illustrations interspersed within the text. Content: Jars, plants, and other objects possibly related to medicinal recipes. Placement: Scattered throughout the manuscript, particularly towards the end.

Overall Style: The illustrations generally exhibit a unique artistic style characterized by a lack of perspective, stylized figures, and non-realistic depictions of plants and objects. The color palette is limited, typically employing greens, blues, browns, and reds. The placement of illustrations often corresponds to the text, suggesting a close relationship between the images and the written content. Recurring motifs, such as nude female figures and circular diagrams, point to potentially symbolic meanings within the manuscript.
