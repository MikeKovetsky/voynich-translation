Here's a summary of prominent theories surrounding the Voynich Manuscript, encompassing its potential decipherments, origins, and intended purposes. Each theory is presented with supporting evidence and references where available.

**1. Linguistic Theories (Decipherment Attempts)**

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is written in a constructed language or code.
    *   **Evidence:** The consistent structure and unique character distribution suggest a deliberate linguistic system rather than random gibberish. Some researchers have attempted to apply linguistic analysis techniques, such as statistical analysis of letter frequencies and patterns, to identify potential grammatical rules and vocabulary.
    *   **Challenges:** No proposed decipherment has gained widespread acceptance. The complex and unusual nature of the script makes it difficult to apply conventional linguistic methods.

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is written in a natural language, obscured by a cipher or code.
    *   **Evidence:** Some researchers propose that the text is a natural language (e.g., Latin, Hebrew, or an archaic dialect) that has been transformed using a complex encryption method. This could involve substitution, transposition, or other cryptographic techniques.
    *   **Challenges:** Successfully deciphering the text requires identifying both the underlying language and the encryption algorithm. This is complicated by the absence of a Rosetta Stone or known key.

**2. Origin and Authorship Theories**

*   **Theory:** The manuscript was created during the Renaissance.
    *   **Evidence:** Carbon dating of the vellum suggests it dates from the early 15th century. The style of the illustrations, particularly the botanical and astronomical drawings, is consistent with artistic conventions of the Renaissance period.
    *   **Counter-evidence:** Some scholars have proposed earlier or later dates based on stylistic analysis.

*   **Theory:** Roger Bacon is the author.
    *   **Evidence:** This theory gained traction due to William Romaine Newbold's early decipherment attempts, which linked the manuscript to Bacon. However, Newbold's decipherment has since been discredited.
    *   **Challenges:** There is no direct evidence linking Bacon to the manuscript. The dating of the vellum also presents a challenge to this theory.

*   **Theory:** Multiple authors collaborated on the manuscript.
    *   **Evidence:** The different sections of the manuscript (e.g., botanical, astronomical, balneological) could suggest different areas of expertise and potentially different authors contributing to the overall work.

**3. Purpose and Function Theories**

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is a herbal or pharmacopoeia.
    *   **Evidence:** The extensive botanical illustrations suggest the manuscript could be a catalog of plants and their medicinal uses. Many of the plants depicted, however, do not match known species.

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is an alchemical or scientific treatise.
    *   **Evidence:** The illustrations of interconnected vessels and astrological diagrams have led to speculation that the manuscript contains alchemical or scientific knowledge. Some researchers propose that it could be a guide to transmutation or other esoteric practices.

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is a hoax or forgery.
    *   **Evidence:** This theory suggests that the manuscript was created as a deliberate deception. It's been proposed that the book was made to fool Rudolf II, who was known to pay top money for rare manuscripts.
    *   **Challenges:** The complexity and scale of the manuscript make this theory less plausible to some. The amount of work required to produce such a detailed and elaborate forgery would have been substantial.

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is a book of secrets or mysteries.
    *   **Evidence:** This theory suggests the manuscript was meant to contain forbidden knowledge. The unfamiliar script and bizarre illustrations add to the mystery.

**4. Other theories:**

*   **Theory:** The manuscript is written in a previously unknown language from Asia.
    *   **Evidence:** The look of the script and organization are similar to languages from Asia.
    *   **Challenges:** There are no experts in language able to read it.
