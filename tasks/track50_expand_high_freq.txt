# Track 50: Expand Dictionary - High Frequency Words

## OBJECTIVE
Analyze the most common UNTRANSLATED words and attempt to decode them.
Target: Add 100+ new entries from high-frequency unknowns.

## INPUT FILES
- `results/hybrid_dictionary.json` - Current 207 entries
- `data/eva_ivtff.txt` - Source text
- `results/grammar_words.json` - Grammar analysis

## BACKGROUND
Current dictionary covers 22.23% of corpus by occurrence.
Many high-frequency words remain untranslated.
Goal: Push coverage to 40%+

## TASKS

### 1. Extract All Unknown High-Frequency Words

Find words that:
- Appear 50+ times in corpus
- Are NOT in current dictionary
- Are NOT obvious variants of known words

### 2. Categorize by Pattern

Group unknowns by structure:
- Words starting with `qo-` (article prefix?)
- Words ending in `-y` (suffix)
- Words ending in `-dy` / `-edy` (verb suffix?)
- Words with `ch-` prefix
- Words with `-aiin` ending

### 3. Context-Based Deduction

For each unknown, analyze:
- What known words appear BEFORE it?
- What known words appear AFTER it?
- Does position suggest grammar function?

Example:
```
If "X" always appears after "heart" and before "-y" suffix:
→ X might be an adjective or verb related to heart
```

### 4. Consonant Skeleton Matching

Try matching unknown word skeletons to:
- Italian botanical terms not yet matched
- Hebrew medical terms not yet matched
- Latin pharmaceutical terms

### 5. Variant Grouping

Many words are variants of the same root:
- `chedy`, `shedy`, `chey`, `shey` → all verb forms?
- `okeedy`, `qokeedy`, `okeey` → same root with prefixes?

Group variants and assign unified meaning.

### 6. Build Expanded Dictionary

For each new entry:
```json
{
  "word": "chedy",
  "meaning": "is/has (verb)",
  "confidence": 0.6,
  "evidence": "appears after subjects, before objects",
  "frequency": 2664,
  "variants": ["shedy", "chey", "shey"]
}
```
