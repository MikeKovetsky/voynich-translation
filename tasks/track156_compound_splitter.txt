Task: Compound Word Splitter

The morphological parser (Task 153) revealed a "Word Soup" problem: many "roots" are actually long strings of multiple words concatenated together (e.g., `aiinchkaiincthor...`).
This task will break these long compounds into atomic roots.

Input:
- results/parsed_text.json (Output of Task 153)

Subtasks:
1. **Identify Atomic Roots:**
   - Scan `parsed_text.json` for roots that are:
     - Short (length 2-5)
     - Frequent (count > 10)
   - Build a "Valid Root List" (e.g., `aiin`, `chol`, `daiin`, `or`).

2. **Split Compounds:**
   - For every root in `parsed_text.json` longer than 6 characters:
     - Try to decompose it into a sequence of Valid Roots.
     - Example: `aiinchol` -> `aiin` + `chol`.
     - Use a greedy left-to-right matching strategy.

3. **Re-assemble Data:**
   - Create `results/parsed_text_v2.json`.
   - Structure: `{"original": "qokaiinchol", "prefix": ["qok"], "roots": ["aiin", "chol"], "suffix": []}`.

4. **Output:**
   - `results/parsed_text_v2.json`: The fully segmented text.
   - `results/compound_analysis.md`: A report on the most common compounds (e.g., `chol-daiin`).

This is the final step before we can analyze the grammar of the sentence structure.
