# Track 23: Verb Discovery by Context

## OBJECTIVE
Find Latin verbs by analyzing word position and context rather than phonetic matching.

## BACKGROUND
Track 19 found 0 high-confidence verb matches using phonetic prediction. This suggests verbs may:
1. Use different encoding than nouns
2. Be heavily abbreviated
3. Appear in specific positions (Latin verbs often sentence-final)

New approach: Find verbs by WHERE they appear, not WHAT they look like.

## TASKS

### 1. Position Analysis
Latin verbs typically appear:
- End of sentence/clause
- After subject noun
- Before "de + noun" phrases (in instructions)

Find words that consistently appear in these positions.

### 2. Medieval Herbal Verb Patterns
Common verb structures in herbals:
- IMPERATIVE: "Recipe" (take), "Misce" (mix), "Coque" (cook)
- INDICATIVE: "Valet" (is effective), "Sanat" (heals), "Curat" (cures)
- PASSIVE: "Datur" (is given), "Bibitur" (is drunk)

Search for words that:
- Start lines (imperatives)
- Follow noun phrases
- Precede "contra" (against) or "ad" (for)

### 3. Frequency Analysis
Verbs should:
- Appear on many pages (common actions)
- Have moderate frequency (not as common as nouns)
- Show limited inflection (unlike nouns with many case forms)

Find words matching this profile.

### 4. Context Window Analysis
For each verb candidate:
- Extract 5-word window around it
- Check if preceding word is noun-like
- Check if following word is preposition-like
- Score based on context consistency

### 5. Abbreviation Hypothesis
If verbs are abbreviated, look for:
- Very short words (2-3 chars) in verb positions
- Words ending in specific markers
- Single-letter words that might be verb abbreviations

### 6. Imperative Search
Medieval recipes use imperatives heavily. Search for:
- Line-initial words
- Words before ingredient lists
- Words that repeat in similar contexts
