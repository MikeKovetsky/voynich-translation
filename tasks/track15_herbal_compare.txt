================================================================================
REFERENCE TEXTS TO COMPARE:
================================================================================

1. MACER FLORIDUS (De Viribus Herbarum, 11th century)
   - ~77 chapters on medicinal plants
   - Written in Latin verse
   - Common phrases: "valet contra...", "prodest ad..."

2. PSEUDO-APULEIUS HERBARIUS (4th-5th century)
   - Illustrated herbal
   - Entry format: name, synonyms, description, uses
   - Phrases: "ad dolorem...", "contra venenum..."

3. DIOSCORIDES LATIN (De Materia Medica translations)
   - 5 books on medicinal substances
   - Greek original, Latin translations from 6th century
   - Structure: name, habitat, description, properties, uses

3. STRUCTURAL COMPARISON
   Medieval herbal entry structure:
   
   A) OPENING: Plant name + "herba/planta est..."
   B) DESCRIPTION: "habet folia...", "crescit in..."
   C) PROPERTIES: "natura calida/frigida", "complexio..."
   D) USES: "valet contra...", "prodest ad...", "curat..."
   E) PREPARATION: "in aqua coquatur", "cum vino bibatur"
   F) DOSAGE: "drachmae duae", "quantum sufficit"
   
   Check if Voynich entries show similar positional patterns.

4. PHRASE MATCHING
   Look for Latin herbal phrases in decoded Voynich:
   - "contra dolorem" (against pain)
   - "ad stomachum" (for stomach)
   - "in aqua" (in water)
   - "cum vino" (with wine)
   - "pro febribus" (for fevers)
   - "valet ad" (is good for)
   - "prodest contra" (helps against)

5. CORNFLOWER (CENTAUREA) SPECIFIC
   Research medieval descriptions of Cornflower:
   - Latin names: Centaurea, Cyanus
   - Medieval uses: eye problems, wounds, fever
   - Expected phrases: "ad oculos", "contra febrem"
   
   Compare with decoded f17r (Cornflower page)
