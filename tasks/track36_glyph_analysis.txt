# Track 36: Original Glyph Pattern Analysis

## OBJECTIVE
Analyze the Voynich script at the GLYPH level, WITHOUT Latin-letter assumptions.
The EVA/Claston transliteration may have introduced artifacts that bias our analysis.

## THE PROBLEM
Our "Latin-like" findings may be transliteration artifacts:

1. EVA assigns Latin letters ARBITRARILY to Voynich glyphs
2. Our phonetic map was designed to produce Latin
3. The "Latin endings" (-us, -ae, -am) may be circular reasoning

Example:
- EVA `aiin` → we decode as `-eiis` (Latin-like!)
- But `aiin` is just how EVA writes certain GLYPH COMBINATIONS
- The "Latin-ness" may be an artifact of EVA design, not the original text

## TASKS

### 1. Catalog Original Glyphs
From Voynich images or glyph tables:
- List all unique glyph shapes
- Count occurrences of each
- Ignore EVA/Latin letter assignments

Reference: https://www.voynich.nu/writing/index.html

Voynich has approximately:
- 20-25 basic glyph shapes
- Several "gallows" characters (tall letters)
- Bench characters
- Ligatures/combinations

### 2. Glyph Position Analysis
For each glyph shape, analyze:
- Initial position frequency
- Medial position frequency  
- Final position frequency
- Does NOT use EVA names, uses glyph IDs (G1, G2, etc.)

### 3. Glyph Combination Patterns
What glyphs frequently appear together?
- Bigram frequencies (glyph pairs)
- Common prefixes (glyph sequences)
- Common suffixes (glyph sequences)

### 4. Compare with Known Scripts
Compare Voynich glyph statistics with:
- Latin script patterns (for reference)
- Arabic script patterns
- Hebrew script patterns
- Invented/constructed scripts
- Shorthand systems (Tironian, etc.)

Key question: Does Voynich resemble ANY known writing system at the glyph level?

### 5. EVA Bias Assessment
Analyze how EVA design may have introduced bias:
- Which glyphs got "vowel-like" letters (a, e, i, o)?
- Which got "consonant-like" letters?
- Did these assignments follow Latin frequency expectations?
- Could different EVA assignments produce "Arabic-like" or "Hebrew-like" results?

### 6. Language-Agnostic Features
Extract features that don't depend on transliteration:
- Word length distribution (in glyphs, not letters)
- Character/glyph diversity per word
- Repetition patterns
- Line-initial vs line-final patterns
