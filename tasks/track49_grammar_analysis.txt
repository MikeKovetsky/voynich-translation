# Track 49: Grammar Word Identification

## OBJECTIVE
Identify the high-frequency "grammar words" (articles, prepositions, verbs).
Without these, we can't form readable sentences even with vocabulary.

## INPUT FILES
- `data/eva_ivtff.txt` - Source text
- `results/cohen_analysis.json` - Context patterns around cohen
- `results/sentence_structure.json` - Sentence patterns (if exists)

## BACKGROUND

From Track 44, words appearing frequently BEFORE/AFTER cohen variants:
- `ol` (349 total) - possibly "the" or "of"
- `chedy`, `shedy` (444 total) - possibly verb forms
- `chey`, `shey` (320 total) - possibly verb forms
- `ar` (124 total) - possibly preposition
- `daiin`, `aiin` (208 total) - possibly grammatical

## TASKS

### 1. Extract High-Frequency Short Words

Find all words with:
- Length 2-4 characters
- Frequency > 100 occurrences
- These are likely grammar words

### 2. Positional Analysis

For each candidate:
- % appearing at line START (likely verbs/imperatives)
- % appearing at line END (likely nouns/objects)
- % appearing BETWEEN content words (likely articles/prepositions)

### 3. Context Analysis

What types of words appear BEFORE and AFTER each candidate?
- If followed by "content words" → likely article/preposition
- If preceded by "content words" → likely verb ending

### 4. Compare to Known Grammar Patterns

Italian articles: il, la, lo, le, un, una
Hebrew articles: ha- (ה), le- (ל), be- (ב)
Latin prepositions: de, ad, cum, per, in

Map candidates to possible grammar functions.

### 5. Build Grammar Hypothesis

| Voynich | Proposed Function | Evidence | Confidence |
|---------|-------------------|----------|------------|
| ol | article "the" | position, frequency | 0.6 |
| ar | preposition | follows nouns | 0.5 |
| chedy | verb ending "-ed" | after subjects | 0.4 |

### 6. Test on Sample Sentences

Take sentences with known vocabulary:
- Insert grammar interpretations
- Does it read more naturally?
