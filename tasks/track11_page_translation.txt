================================================================================
SPECIFIC TASKS:
================================================================================

1. EXTRACT PAGE TEXT
   - Get all text from f17r (or f5r as backup)
   - Format: <17r.1>text <17r.2>text etc.
   - Parse into individual words
   - Count: total words, unique words

2. PARSE EACH WORD GRAMMATICALLY
   For every word on the page:
   - Identify prefix (4o-, 1-, 8-, o-, or none)
   - Identify root (middle portion)
   - Identify suffix (-9, -89, -am, -oe, -ay, -an, etc.)
   
   Create table:
   | Word | Prefix | Root | Suffix | Proposed Function |
   |------|--------|------|--------|-------------------|

3. APPLY PHONETIC MAPPING
   Use best mapping from other tracks (or create Latin-based mapping):
   
   Proposed mapping (Latin frequency):
   - o → a/o (most common)
   - 9 → s/is (nominative ending)
   - 4 → qu
   - h → r (common consonant)
   - c → c/k
   - 8 → d/t
   - a → a
   - m → m
   - e → e
   - etc.

   Apply to each word → get "decoded" form

4. ATTEMPT TRANSLATION
   Using grammar rules + phonetic mapping:
   
   a) Translate sentence by sentence
   b) For each sentence:
      - Identify subject (nominative case)
      - Identify verb (1- prefix words)
      - Identify object (accusative case)
      - Identify modifiers (genitive, locative)
   
   c) Produce English/Latin gloss:
      - "4ohan 8am 1c89" → "the-herb.ABL of-it root.GEN.PL" → "from the herb, of its roots"

5. COMPARE WITH MEDIEVAL HERBAL STRUCTURE
   Typical medieval herbal entry structure:
   
   1. Plant name
   2. Physical description (leaves, flowers, roots)
   3. Habitat (where it grows)
   4. Medicinal properties (hot/cold, wet/dry)
   5. Uses (for what ailments)
   6. Preparation (how to use)
   
   Does our translation follow this pattern?

6. ASSESS COHERENCE
   Rate the translation:
   - Grammatical coherence (do sentences parse?)
   - Semantic coherence (do meanings make sense?)
   - Domain coherence (does it sound like herbal text?)
   - Cross-reference with known Cornflower/Hellebore properties
