Task: Unsupervised Morphological Segmentation

Our dictionary coverage is low because we treat `chol`, `ychol`, `cholody` as three different words. They are likely variations of the same root.

Input Files:
- data/voynich_transcription.txt (Raw text)

Subtasks:
1. **Affix Stripping:**
   - Write a script to identify the most common start-sequences (prefixes) and end-sequences (suffixes) in the text.
   - Targets: `qo-`, `y-`, `o-`, `-dy`, `-ol`, `-y`.
   - Algorithm:
     - For every word type W:
       - Try splitting W into P+Root and Root+S.
       - Count how often P and S appear as affixes elsewhere.
       - If P and S are frequent, propose "Root" as a new dictionary candidate.

2. **Root consolidation:**
   - Apply these rules to the entire vocabulary.
   - Create a mapping `derived_word -> root`.
   - Example: `ychol` -> `chol`, `cholody` -> `chol`.

3. **Coverage Update:**
   - Count how many "unknown" words in our dictionary can be reduced to "known" roots using these rules.

Output:
- results/morphology_rules.json: The discovered prefixes/suffixes.
- results/root_mapping.json: Map of complex words to roots.
- results/coverage_report.md: How much does this increase our "virtual" coverage?
