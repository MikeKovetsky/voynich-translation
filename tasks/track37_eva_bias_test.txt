# Track 37: EVA Transliteration Bias Test

## OBJECTIVE
Test whether our "Latin-like" findings are artifacts of how EVA was designed,
or genuine properties of the Voynich script.

## THE HYPOTHESIS
EVA designers may have (consciously or unconsciously) assigned Latin letters
to Voynich glyphs in ways that produce Latin-like statistics.

## EXPERIMENT DESIGN

### 1. Create Alternative Transliterations
Design 3-4 alternative letter assignments that are equally valid:

**Alternative A: "Arabic-biased"**
Assign letters to glyphs to maximize Arabic-like patterns:
- High-frequency glyphs → Arabic vowels
- Gallows → Arabic emphatics
- etc.

**Alternative B: "Hebrew-biased"**  
Assign letters to maximize Hebrew-like patterns

**Alternative C: "Random"**
Random but consistent letter assignment

**Alternative D: "Inverted"**
Swap vowels ↔ consonants from EVA

### 2. Re-analyze with Each System
For each alternative transliteration:
- Calculate letter frequencies
- Find common endings
- Run language comparison (Track 33 style)
- Calculate "Latin-like" score

### 3. Compare Results
| Transliteration | Latin Score | Arabic Score | Hebrew Score |
|-----------------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| EVA (original)  | 45% | ? | ? |
| Alternative A   | ? | ? | ? |
| Alternative B   | ? | ? | ? |
| Random          | ? | ? | ? |

### 4. Statistical Test
If EVA is unbiased:
- Latin score should be similar across all transliterations
- Or at least, EVA shouldn't be HIGHEST for Latin

If EVA is biased:
- EVA will show highest Latin score
- Alternatives will show lower Latin scores
- "Latin-like" finding is an artifact
