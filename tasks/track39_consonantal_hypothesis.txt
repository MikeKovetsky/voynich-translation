# Track 39: Consonantal Writing Hypothesis

## OBJECTIVE
Test whether Voynich uses a CONSONANTAL writing system where vowels are
omitted or minimally represented (like Hebrew, Arabic, or medieval shorthand).

## BACKGROUND
Track 37 found Hebrew (0.415) and Arabic (0.373) score higher than Latin (0.213).
Both Hebrew and Arabic are consonantal scripts - vowels are often not written.

If Voynich is consonantal:
- "Vowel" glyphs may actually be consonants
- Or vowels are represented differently than we think
- This would explain why "Latin" decoding doesn't produce readable words

## INPUT FILES
- `results/glyph_analysis.json` - Glyph inventory
- `results/eva_bias_test.json` - Transliteration tests
- `data/eva_ivtff.txt` - Source transcription

## TASKS

### 1. Vowel/Consonant Distribution Analysis
In EVA, "vowels" are: o, a, e, i (assigned to high-frequency glyphs)

Test:
- What if these are actually consonants?
- What's the vowel:consonant ratio in Voynich?
- Compare with:
  - Latin: ~38% vowels
  - Hebrew text: ~0% written vowels (consonants only)
  - Arabic: ~10% vowels when written

### 2. Consonant Cluster Analysis
Consonantal scripts allow consonant clusters that would be impossible in Latin.

Search Voynich for:
- Long sequences of "consonant" glyphs
- Patterns that violate Latin phonotactics
- Evidence of missing vowels

- Need to look for root patterns
