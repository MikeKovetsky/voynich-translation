# Track 35: Voynich Alphabet Verification

## OBJECTIVE
Verify that our understanding of the Voynich alphabet is correct and complete.

## BACKGROUND
The Voynich manuscript uses a unique script. Multiple transcription systems exist:
- EVA (European Voynich Alphabet) - most widely used
- Claston (Glen Claston's system) - uses numbers
- FSG (First Study Group) - historical
- Currier - historical

If we misunderstand ANY character, all decoding fails.

## INPUT FILES
- `data/eva_ivtff.txt` - EVA transcription
- `voynich_raw.txt` - Claston transcription
- `results/transcription_mapping.json` - Our current mapping
- Web sources for EVA definition

## TASKS

### 1. Extract Complete Character Sets
From both transcription files, extract:
- All unique characters used
- Frequency of each character
- Position distribution (initial/medial/final)

### 2. Verify Against Official EVA
Cross-reference with official EVA definition:
- https://www.voynich.nu/extra/eva.html
- Are all EVA characters present?
- Are there unknown characters?

### 3. Verify Claston Mapping
For each Claston character:
- What is the official EVA equivalent?
- Do our mappings match published mappings?
- Are there any errors?

### 4. Check for "Weirdos"
EVA has special "weirdo" characters for rare glyphs:
- Are we handling these correctly?
- Are they being filtered or kept?
- Could important information be lost?

### 5. Digraph Verification
Some characters are digraphs in EVA:
- `ch` (gallows)
- `sh` (gallows) 
- `cth` (tall gallows)
- `ckh` (bench-gallows)

Verify:
- Are we parsing digraphs correctly?
- Are word boundaries correct around digraphs?

### 6. Rare Character Analysis
For characters appearing <100 times:
- What are they?
- Are they errors/variants/meaningful?
- Should they be normalized?
