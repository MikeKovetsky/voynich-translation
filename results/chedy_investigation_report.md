## 5. Conclusion
1.  **Not a Specific Plant Label:** The absence of `chedy` in herbal labels strongly suggests it is **not** the name of a specific plant (e.g., "Rose", "Sage").
2.  **Generic Noun ("Herb" or "Mixture"):** Its high frequency in recipes (181 times) and position in phrases like `daiin chedy` ("Take [the] herb/mixture") and `qokchedy` ("With/Cook [the] herb/mixture") supports a generic meaning.
3.  **Process vs Result:**
    - `chedy qokeey` (Herb -> Cook) implies it's an ingredient being processed.
    - Its appearance at the *end* of recipes (tied for most common ending word) suggests it can also refer to the final product ("The Mixture" or "The Potion") or an instruction ("Drink"/"Serve").
4.  **Recommendation:** Treat `chedy` as **"Herb/Mixture"** (Noun) primarily. The "Drink" hypothesis is weaker because `chedy qokeey` ("Drink cook"?) is syntactically awkward unless `qokeey` is "Process".
