## 4. Hypothesis Testing

### H1: Same Plant (f9r is a "bad drawing" of Nettle)
**Evidence:** `choly` appears on both.
**Counter-Evidence:** Visual identification of f9r strongly suggests Oak (leaf shape) or Pansy (flower). Text similarity is low (0.15).
**Verdict:** Unlikely. The texts are too different, and visual mismatch is strong.

### H2: Shared Name / Category ("Nettle" is a category)
**Evidence:** Both pages contain `choly`. `chol` is also present on both.
**Analysis:** `choly` might not mean "Nettle" specifically, but a property like "stinging", "serrated leaves" (Oak leaves are lobed/serrated?), or a medicinal property.
**Verdict:** Plausible. `choly` could describe a shared attribute (e.g., "bitter", "rough", "medicinal usage").

### H3: Misidentification
**Evidence:** f28v is widely accepted as Nettle. f9r is debated.
**Verdict:** If `choly` implies Nettle, then f9r is either a Nettle or `choly` means something else. Given the visual of f9r (Oak-like), it is more likely that `choly` describes a property shared by Oak and Nettle (e.g., pharmacological effect or texture) rather than taxomonical identity.

## 5. Summary
The presence of `choly` on both f9r and f28v is confirmed as an exact match. However, the overall textual context is dissimilar. This supports the hypothesis that `choly` is an attribute or category marker rather than a unique plant identifier for just one species, or that f9r shares a specific property with Nettle.
