# Ordaiin Profile

## 1. Introduction
`ordaiin` is one of the most significant terms in the Voynich Manuscript, appearing frequently in the recipes and as a label in the Herbal Section. Its high frequency and strategic placement suggest it represents a key ingredient, quality, or plant.

## 3. Visual Correlation
The term `ordaiin` (often as a suffix or root component in labels) appears on pages depicting various plants, not just one.
- **f2v (Water Lily):** Aquatic plant, often associated with the moon or water, but yellow varieties exist.
- **f9v (Pansy):** *Viola tricolor* has purple, yellow ("gold"), and white petals.
- **f25v (Plantain):** A healing herb, not visually "golden" but highly medicinal.
- **f93r (Sunflower):** The term `ordaiin` does **not** appear as a primary label on the famous "Sunflower" page (f93r), though related forms like `odaiin` (root `aiin`, noun `od`) are present.

**Conclusion:** `ordaiin` is likely **not** the name of a single specific plant like the Sunflower. Instead, it appears to be a **generic term, quality, or substance** present in many plants (e.g., "The Golden Essence", "The Extract", "The Root").

## 4. Etymological Analysis
- **Structure:** `or` + `daiin`.
- **Prefix `or`:** Strongly suggests **"Gold"** (Latin/Romance *aurum*, French/Catalan *or*).
- **Root `daiin`:** One of the most common words in the VMS.
- **Interpretation:**
    - **"Golden Daiin":** If `daiin` = "Star", "Grain", or "Element", then `ordaiin` = "Golden Grain" or "Golden Element".
    - **"Gold of...":** If `daiin` is a modifier.
    - **"Potable Gold" (*Aurum Potabile*):** A legendary alchemical medicine. Given its frequency in recipes and presence on medicinal plants (Plantain, Pansy, Hellebore), `ordaiin` may refer to the **universal medicinal active principle** extracted from these plants.

## 5. Final Hypothesis
`ordaiin` identifies the **"Golden Ingredient"** or **"Universal Medicine"**. It is not a single botanical species but a chemical or alchemical property derived from multiple sources, particularly those on pages f2v, f3v, f9v, and f25v.
