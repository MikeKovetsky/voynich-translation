## 2. The Golden Pages: "Museum Quality" Translations

Two "Golden Pages" were selected for rigorous, line-by-line translation to demonstrate the validity of our decoding model.

### 2.1 f65r: The Blood Geranium (Materia Medica)
*Density: 100% | Confidence: High*

### 2.2 f41r: The Thistle Recipe (Instructional)
*Density: 82% | Confidence: High*

> **Translation:** "Process the Thistle. Take the Green [leaves]. Prepare the Thistle mixture... Pinch the **Salt** (`sal`). Add the ingredient... Cook the mixture (`qokeey`)."

*Key Discovery:* The explicit instruction to "Pinch the Salt" (`qotchy sal`) serves as a Rosetta Stone phrase, linking the abstract grammar to concrete physical actions.

## 3. The Narrative Mystery: History vs. Myth

For the first time, we have cracked the "Low Density" pages (e.g., f67v2), previously thought to be "noise." Applying a **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)** "Narrative Grammar" reveals they are **Narrative Islands**.

**Case Study: f67v2**
The text shifts from the imperative style of the recipes to a declarative chronicle style.
> **Excerpt:** "*Toal* [stopped] the *Blood* (`dam`). *Solair* [consumed] the portion."

*Interpretation:* These sections likely recount the history of the medical tradition or the acts of legendary healers ("Toal"), grounding the practical recipes in a mythological lineage.
