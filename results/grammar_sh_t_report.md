## 4. Interpretation & Conclusions

### `sh-` as "That/Which" (Relative Pronoun)
- **High Frequency in Narrative**: The Bio section (Narrative) has the highest frequency of `sh-` words (10.81%), suggesting highly descriptive text ("The plant **that** is red...", "**Which** grows in...").
- **Attaches to Nouns/Objects**: `shol` (That + The/It?), `shaiin` (That + Water?).
- **Absence with `dain`**: `shdain` (That + Take) is **non-existent** (0 vs 156). This strongly supports `dain` being an **Imperative Verb** ("Take!"). One does not say "That take". If `sh-` was a generic definite article ("The"), we might expect "The taking" or similar, but its total absence suggests a grammatical incompatibility.
- **`shedy` Anomaly**: `shedy` is very common, but `edy` is 0. This suggests `shedy` might be a distinct root, or `sh-` implies a specific state for `edy` that is the default form in this text. Alternatively, `e` is part of the prefix (`she-dy` -> `dy` exists).

### `t-` as "To/Future" (Preposition/Tense)
- **Low Frequency**: `t-` is much rarer (0.5% - 1.6%) than `sh-`.
- **Specific Association**: Strongly associates with `ch-` words (`tchol`, `tchedy`) and `sh-` words (`tshedy`).
- **`tchol` vs `chol`**: `tchol` appears in contexts with `chol`, possibly indicating movement or state change ("From **chol** to **tchol**"). If `chol` is "Whole/All/Light", `tchol` could be "To the Whole" or "Will be Whole".
- **`tshedy`**: `t-` can precede `sh-`. `t-sh-edy`. If `sh-` is "That", `t-` is "To", then `tshedy` = "To that which is...". This stacking supports the prefix hypothesis.

### Summary
- **`sh-`**: Confirmed as a likely **Relative Particle** ("That/Which"). Its distribution matches a descriptive function and it avoids imperative verbs like `dain`.
- **`t-`**: Likely a **Preposition (To)** or **Future/Modal Marker**. It can stack on top of `sh-`, indicating it operates at a higher phrase level (Preposition + Relative Clause).
