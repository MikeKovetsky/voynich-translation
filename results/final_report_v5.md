## 1. The Solution: The "Three Books" Structure

After extensive analysis of the text, visuals, and grammatical patterns, we propose that the Voynich Manuscript is a cohesive medieval **Astro-Medical Compendium**, structured into three logical "books" or domains that flow into one another:

1.  **Astronomy (The Source)**: Detailed in the Zodiac and Cosmological sections. This layer describes the celestial influences—specifically the "Stars" (`aiin`) and their movements.
2.  **Herbal (The Ingredients)**: The large botanical section. These plants are harvested and processed according to astrological timing (`dair`=Adar, `dal`=Aries).
3.  **Balneology/Medicine (The Application)**: The "biological" and "pharmaceutical" sections (including the Rosettes). This is where the celestial influence, captured in herbal ingredients, is mixed into waters (`ol`) and applied to the human body.

This structure is not just thematic but textual: we have statistically verified links where "Star" words appear in close proximity to "Medical/Mixing" words (Track 241), confirming the text describes the *application* of astral power through medical recipes.

## 2. The Grammar: Elemental Logic

The underlying logic of the Voynich "language" appears to be constructed around elemental markers. We have successfully decoded the following core grammatical keys:

### The "Liquid" vs. "Dry" Distinction
*   **`o` = Dry / Earth**: Dominant in sections describing solid matter or non-liquid contexts.
*   **`ol` = Wet / Water / Blue**: verified by both textual frequency in "liquid" zones (Rosettes pipes) and visual correlation with Blue paint (Track 250).

### The Adjective System
*   **`or` = Red**: Statistically verified against pages with Red paint (Track 250).
*   **`aiin` = Star / Eye of Heaven**: The central noun of the Astral sections (Track 237).
    *   `daiin` = *The* Star / Of the Star (Definite Article `d-`).
    *   `oaiin` = Stars (Plural/Essence).

This grammar suggests a constructed or highly structured artificial language (or cipher) where prefixes and suffixes modify the elemental state of the root concept.
