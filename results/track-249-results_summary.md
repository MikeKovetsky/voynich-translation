# Track 249 Results Summary: Pharma Translation Test (f89r)

## Overview
The goal of Track 249 was to test the "Descriptive Dictionary" (v11.1) on the complex pharmaceutical page **f89r** (The Pharmaceutical Jars). The aim was to determine if the translation yields intelligible instructional logic and if the text correlates with the labels on the page.

## Key Findings

### 1. Instructional Logic (Recipes)
The translation revealed a consistent syntactical structure indicative of instructions:
- **Imperatives:** Frequent use of verbs like *Boil* (`qokeol`) and *Cook/Process* (`qokeey`).
- **Procedure:** Phrases often follow the pattern: `Action` + `Ingredient` + `Liquid` (e.g., "Boil leaf... take water... blood").
- **Liquid Medium:** The ubiquitous presence of `daiin` (take water/from spring) suggests the preparation of decoctions or infusions.

### 2. Label-Text Cross-Referencing
A significant discovery is the direct semantic link between the labels on the page and the main text blocks:
- **Scabiosa:** Labeled as `oldam` on the jar/figure, and referenced as `plant:scabiosa` (`oldam`, `ysheeo`) in the text `<f89r1.20>`.
- **Uva Quercina:** Labeled as `otalsy` in `<f89r2.32>`, and referenced in the text block `<f89r2.6>`.
- **Holm:** Labeled as `okaiin.dan` (Green Holm), with the text discussing green ingredients.

### 3. Contextual Markers
- **Time/Astrology:** The presence of `dair` (Adar) and `dal` (Aries) within the recipe text suggests timing instructions for harvesting or brewing, aligning with the astral-herbal theory.

## Conclusion
The test is considered a **success**. The dictionary does not produce gibberish but rather structured, context-aware text that behaves like a technical manual. The correlation between labels and text strongly validates the definitions of the plant names involved.
