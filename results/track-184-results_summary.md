# Task 184 Results Summary

## Overview
Validated the color words (`or`/`ok`=Green, `ot`=Red) against plant part words (`chol`=Leaf, `shor`=Root) on 10 herbal pages (`f10r` to `f17r`).

## Findings
- **Matches:** 4
- **Mismatches:** 9
- **Ratio:** 4/13 (30.8%)

## Detailed Observations
- **Leaf (`chol`):**
    - Often appeared near "Red" (`ot-`) words (e.g., f10r, f11v, f15v, f16v), leading to mismatches.
    - Some matches with "Green" (`or`/`ok-`) were found on f13r.
- **Root (`shor`):**
    - Showed matches with "Red" on f10r.
    - One mismatch with "Green" on f15r.
    - Frequently appeared with both color types nearby (Ambiguous), suggesting complex context.

## Conclusion
The hypothesis that `or`/`ok-` exclusively signifies "Green" for Leaves and `ot-` signifies "Red" for Roots is **not strongly supported** by this sample (30% accuracy).
- The high frequency of "Red" words near "Leaf" (`chol`) suggests either:
    1. `ot-` is not "Red" (or `chol` is not "Leaf").
    2. The text describes "Red Leaves" (which exists in some illustrations).
    3. The color words function differently (e.g., they might be grammatical markers rather than adjectives).
- **Recommendation:** Re-evaluate the color word mapping or the syntactic relationship (e.g., are they prepositions "from the root" vs "red root"?).
