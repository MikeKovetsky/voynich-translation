# Task 172 Results Summary: The `ed` Root Investigation

## Executive Summary
The `ed` root (appearing primarily as `edy`, `qokedy`, `otedy`) is a fundamental term in the **Biological** and **Recipe** sections of the manuscript. Its morphological profile—heavily suffixed with `-y` and frequently prefixed with `qok-` (the Verb Marker)—suggests it functions primarily as a **verb or participle** denoting a process or state, likely related to **mixing, processing, or biological activity**.

## Key Findings

### 1. Distribution
- **Dominant Sections:**
    - **Biological:** 490 occurrences (43%)
    - **Recipes:** 429 occurrences (37%)
    - **Herbal B:** 126 occurrences (11%)
    - *Note:* It is virtually absent in Astronomical (4) and Herbal A (2) sections, marking a sharp vocabulary divide.

### 2. Morphology
- **Root Stability:** The `ed` root almost always appears with the suffix `-y` (1082/1143 cases), suggesting the canonical form is `edy`.
- **Verb Marker (`qok-`):** This is the most common prefix (279 occurrences), strongly implying a verbal function (e.g., "to [ed]", "being [ed]").
- **Other Prefixes:** `che-` (129), `she-` (123), `oke-` (118), `ote-` (107). The noun prefix `o-` is rare (15), though `ote-` and `oke-` might function similarly.

### 3. Semantic Associations & Collocations
- **Proxies:**
    - Strong co-occurrence with **"Air"** proxies (`ol`, `or`): 128 hits.
    - Strong co-occurrence with **"Stone/Earth"** proxies (`al`, `ar`): 117 hits.
    - Moderate association with **"Mix/Process"** terms.
- **Context:** Frequently flanked by `shedy`, `chedy`, `daiin`, `ol`. The recursive pattern (e.g., `qokedy` ... `qokedy`) suggests repetitive instructions or continuous processes.

## Conclusion: The Meaning of `ed`
Based on its "Recipe/Bio" exclusivity and "Verb" morphology, `ed` is likely **not** a simple object like "Star" or "Plant".
**Hypothesis:** `ed` (or `edy`) represents a **core process** or **substance state** in the Voynich alchemy/biology.
- **Candidates:** "Mix", "Heat", "Drink", "Liquid", "Essence".
- **Verdict:** Given the "Air" and "Earth" associations, it might be a "connector" verb like "combine" or a state like "mixed". The absence in Astro/Herbal A reinforces that it is specific to the *preparation* or *biological* narrative, not general observation.
