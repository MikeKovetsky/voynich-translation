# Track 154 Results: Prefix Logic & Grammar

## Overview
This task analyzed the behavior of the prefixes isolated in Task 153 to determine their grammatical functions.

## Key Findings

### 1. Prefix Functions (Hypotheses)
*   **`qo-` / `qok-` (Verb Prefix):**
    *   **Behavior:** Internal position (only 9.9% line-initial).
    *   **Function:** **Process Marker** or "To [Action]". Matches the "Process Verb" theory from the Recipe Section (`qokeey` = To Cook).
*   **`y-` (Conjunction):**
    *   **Behavior:** High line-start frequency (34.0%). Connects phrases.
    *   **Function:** **"And" / "Then"**. Confirmed usage as a connector between ingredients or recipe steps.
*   **`d-` / `dai-` (Imperative/Prep):**
    *   **Behavior:** 21% line-initial. High frequency in the form `daiin`.
    *   **Function:** **"Take" (Imperative)** or Preposition ("Of"). `daiin` accounts for 849 occurrences (Take Water).
*   **`o-` (Determiner):**
    *   **Behavior:** Extremely common (8285 uses), low positional constraint (15.5% start).
    *   **Function:** **"The" (Definite Article)** or Object Marker.
*   **`s-` / `sh-` (Relative):**
    *   **Behavior:** 16.5% line-initial.
    *   **Function:** **"That/Which" (Relative Pronoun)**.

### 2. Root Behavior
*   **Prefix-Phobic Roots:** Some short roots (`a`, `c`, `d`) never take prefixes, suggesting they are function words themselves.
*   **Suffix-Obligatory Roots:** Roots like `alk`, `alshe` always appear with suffixes, suggesting they are bound morphemes.

## Conclusion
The prefixes are **not** random. They exhibit distinct grammatical behaviors:
*   `qo-` is a **Bound Morpheme** (Verb Prefix).
*   `y-` is a **Free Morpheme** (Conjunction) often attached in writing.
*   `o-` is a ubiquitous **Article**.

This structure supports the "Agglutinative/Synthetic" morphological model of Voynich.
