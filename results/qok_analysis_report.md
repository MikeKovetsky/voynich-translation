# Qok- Prefix Analysis Report

## 1. Executive Summary
The `qok-` prefix is a defining characteristic of the Biological Section (Quire 13). Our analysis suggests that `qok-` is likely a compound prefix: **`q-` + `ok-`**.
- **`ok-`** appears to be a derivational prefix (possibly "fluid" or "mixture") that turns roots into Bio/Recipe-specific terms.
- **`q-`** appears to be a prepositional or case prefix (likely "with", "in", or "by") attached to these `ok-` terms.

## 3. Contextual Usage
*   **Section Specificity:** `qok-` words are 3-5x more frequent in the **Biological** section than in Herbal or Pharmaceutical sections.
*   **Preceding Words:**
    *   `shedy` (which/that) - 92 occurrences
    *   `chedy` (plant) - 60 occurrences
    *   Other `qok-` words - 168 occurrences (suggesting lists or parallel structures)
*   **Lists:** 14.2% of `qok-` words immediately follow another `qok-` word, confirming their use in lists of ingredients, states, or processes.

## 4. Hypothesis Evaluation

### Hypothesis A: `qok-` = "with/using" (Instrumental) - **SUPPORTED (as `q-`)**
The morphology suggests `q-` is an attachment to `ok-` words. Given the context of recipes and bathing, a function like "with" (Instrumental) or "in" (Locative) fits perfectly.
*   Usage: `shedy qokeedy` -> "which (is) in/with `okeedy`"
*   Usage: `chedy qokaiin` -> "plant with `okaiin`"

### Hypothesis B: `qok-` = "fluid/water" (Semantic) - **REFINED**
It is more likely that **`ok-`** is the semantic marker for "fluid/liquid/mixture", rather than the full `qok-`.
*   `okeedy` = "the liquid mixture"
*   `q-okeedy` = "in the liquid mixture"

### Hypothesis C: Plural Marker - **WEAK**
The statistical distribution does not resemble a simple plural marker. The `ok-` derivation seems more lexical (changing meaning) than grammatical (plural).

## 5. Recommendation
*   **Treat `q-` as a prefix:** In future translation attempts, try stripping `q-` to see if the remainder is a noun/verb.
*   **Investigate `ok-`:** Focus analysis on what `ok-` does to a root (e.g., `chedy` (plant) -> `okchedy` (plant extract? plant water?)).
