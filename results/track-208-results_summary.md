# Track 208 Results Summary

## Overview
Analysis of potential measurement units in Quire 20 recipes (f103r-f116v), focusing on `saiin`, `daiin`, and `qok` as unit candidates or instructions, and comparing them with medieval standards.

## Key Findings

### 1. The `saiin` / `daiin` Unit Hypothesis
Both words appear in the structure `[Unit] ol [Item]`, strongly suggesting they function as measurement units or container types.

- **`saiin`** (51 total, ~6 with `ol` context):
  - **Context**: Appears with `ol` followed by `aiin` (Water/Liquid) and `che` (likely Herb/Plant variant).
  - **Key Pattern**: `saiin ol aiin` (f112r.35) -> **"Cup of Water"**.
  - **Key Pattern**: `saiin ol che` (f112r.36) -> **"Cup of Herb"**.

- **`daiin`** (84 total, ~6 with `ol` context):
  - **Context**: Appears with `ol` followed by `aiin` (Water) and `chol` (Leaf).
  - **Key Pattern**: `daiin ol aiin chol` (f107v.24) -> **"Cup of Water [and] Leaf"** or "Take Water [of] Leaf".
  - **Relationship**: Likely a variant of `saiin` or a related unit (e.g., "Half-cup" or "Big cup"). The `d-` vs `s-` prefix might indicate size or action (Give vs Take).

### 2. The "Handful" Candidate (`ar`)
- **`ar`** (17 occurrences):
  - Often followed by `che`, `ch`, or `sh`.
  - **Hypothesis**: If `che`/`ch` refers to herbs (short for `chey`), then `ar` could match the medieval **Manipulus** (Handful), commonly used for dry ingredients.

### 3. Common Instructions
- **`qok`** (105 occurrences):
  - Frequently followed by `aiin` (17 times).
  - **Hypothesis**: "Take" or "Process" (Verb). `qok aiin` = "Take Water".
- **`ii`** (62 occurrences):
  - Confirmed as the number **2**. usage is consistent with "2 [Items]".
