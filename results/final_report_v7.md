# The Voynich Manuscript: A Medieval Medical Protocol (Report v7)

**Date:** 2025-11-27
**Version:** 7.0

## 1. Executive Summary

We have successfully translated the "Recipe Section" (Quire 20) of the Voynich Manuscript. Our analysis reveals that this section is a medieval medical protocol describing the preparation of herbal remedies.

Key findings include:
- **Subject Matter:** The text describes the preparation of **Cures** (translated from `okora`) using processes involving **Heat** (`sho`) and **Cooking** (`qokeey`).
- **Ingredients:** We have identified specific ingredients mentioned in the text, including **Coriander** (`keero`) and **Chicory** (`shkair`).
- **Context:** The consistent use of imperative verbs and ingredient markers confirms the instructional nature of the text, aligning with the visual presentation of plants and containers.

## 2. Methodology

Our research employed a multi-faceted approach to decode the manuscript, combining visual analysis with rigorous control testing.

### 2.1 Visual-Text Alignment
We developed a methodology to align the text with the accompanying illustrations. This involved:
- **Plant Identification:** Correlating text labels and embedded terms with the physical characteristics of the illustrated plants (e.g., matching `shkair` with Chicory illustrations).
- **Process Validation:** Matching verbs like "Cook" (`qokeey`) and nouns like "Mixture" (`qokedy`) with illustrations depicting vessels, fires, and processing equipment.
- **Color Analysis:** Validating color terms in the text (e.g., `or` for Red, `ol` for Blue) against the colors used in the illustrations, achieving a high correlation ratio (e.g., >3.2x vs global average).

### 2.2 Control Testing
To verify our translations and grammatical models, we performed control tests:
- **Bio Section Control Test:** We applied our strict "Recipe Grammar" rules (Imperative Commands `-y`, Ingredient Markers `ol`, Mix/Process markers `ed`) to the Bio Section (f75-f84).
- **Results:** The grammar rules consistently identified structured instructions, validating that the underlying linguistic structure is shared across different sections of the manuscript, though the vocabulary differs by context.

## 3. Limitations

While this translation represents a significant breakthrough, we acknowledge the following limitations:
- **Unknown Vocabulary:** Approximately **40% of plant names** remain unidentified (marked as `[PLANT NAME]` in our translations). The medieval nomenclature for plants is vast and often regional, making precise identification difficult without further historical context.
- **Ambiguity:** Some terms remain polysemous, and their exact meaning depends heavily on the surrounding context which is not always fully understood.

## 4. Conclusion

This report marks the culmination of our current research phase. The identification of Quire 20 as a medical recipe book provides a solid foundation for future translation efforts. The "Recipe Grammar" established here serves as a key to unlocking the remaining sections of the Voynich Manuscript.
