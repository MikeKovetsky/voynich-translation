# Visual Confirmation of Measurement Units (Track 212)

## 1. Methodology
- **Text Localization**: Scripted analysis of `results/quire20_translation_master.md` to identify precise locations (Folio, Recipe/Paragraph) of `saiin` (Cup candidate) and `ar` (Handful candidate).
- **Visual Inspection**: Web search and literature review of Voynich Quire 20 (f103r-f116v) marginalia and illustrations.
- **Structural Correlation**: Mapping of text occurrences to page layout features (marginal stars).

## 2. Visual Findings: Quire 20 Margins
**Hypothesis**: Margins near `saiin` or `ar` contain drawings of containers (cups) or hands.

**Observation**:
- **No Containers/Hands**: The margins of Quire 20 (f103r-f116v) do **not** contain drawings of cups, jars, vessels, or hands.
- **Marginal Stars**: The dominant and consistent marginal feature in this section is a series of "stars" (typically 7-8 pointed, often with tails, colored red/yellow).
- **Contrast**: This contrasts with the "Pharmaceutical" section (Quire 19, e.g., f88-f89), which is known to feature drawings of pharmaceutical jars (albarellos) and containers.

## 3. Structural Association (The "Star" Connection)
While no literal drawings of cups were found, a significant **structural correlation** was identified:

### `saiin` / `daiin` and Paragraph Markers
- **Pattern**: `saiin` (and its variant `daiin`) frequently appears as the **first word** of a paragraph.
- **Visual Link**: In Quire 20, paragraphs are almost distinctively marked by the marginal "stars".
- **Implication**:
    - The "Star" visual acts as a bullet point.
    - `saiin` (at the start) acts as the label or initiator for that bullet point.
    - **Interpretation**: `saiin` could mean "Recipe", "Item", "Take", or a specific unit like "Cup" (as in "Cup of [Ingredient]...") that initiates the instruction.
    - The absence of a cup *drawing* suggests `saiin` is a standard unit or instruction understood by the reader, or the "star" itself is an abstract symbol for the unit/item.

**Example Locations (First Word):**
- **f103r (Recipe #8)**: `sain herb/plant...`
- **f103v (Recipe #64)**: `sai!n measure/portion...`
- **f106r (Recipe #285)**: `sai!n oi!n...`
- **f112r (Recipe #713)**: `sai!n The spring...`

### `ar` (Handful)
- **Pattern**: `ar` (and `aral`) typically appears in the **middle** of sentences/recipes.
- **Visual Link**: No specific marginal drawing (like a hand) is associated with `ar` lines.
- **Glyph Analysis**: The glyph `r` has a trailing flourish, but this is a script feature, not a distinct pictorial "hand".
- **Interpretation**: `ar` likely functions as a secondary unit (e.g., "add a handful of...") embedded within the instruction, rather than an initiator.

## 4. Conclusion
- **Direct Visual Confirmation**: **Negative**. No literal drawings of cups (`saiin`) or hands (`ar`) exist in the margins of Quire 20.
- **Structural Confirmation**: **Positive**. `saiin` is structurally privileged as a paragraph-initial term corresponding to the marginal "star" markers. This supports its role as a primary unit, header, or instruction (like "Rx" or "Cup").
- **Recommendation**: Treat `saiin` as the primary "Item/Unit" marker for these recipes, with the "Star" as its visual bullet point.
