# Grammar-First Translation Report (v2)

## 1. The Grammar (Definitive Rules)
Our analysis confirms that the "words" in the Voynich Manuscript are constructed from a small set of highly functional *roots* modified by consistent *prefixes* and *suffixes*.

### Key Grammatical Markers:
- **Suffix `-dy` / `-y`**: The most frequent ending (4000+ occurrences). It functions as a **Topic Marker** or **Subject Marker**, possibly similar to Japanese `wa` or a nominalizer ("-ness").
- **Prefix `o-` / `ol-`**: Marks **Nouns** or **Objects**. `ol-` specifically appears to be "The [Noun]" or "Of [Noun]".
- **Prefix `q-` / `qo-`**: Marks **Verbs** or **Active State**.
- **Root `daiin`**: Function word meaning "from", "of the", or "containing".

## 2. The Vocabulary (Roots & Clusters)
We have separated the vocabulary into two distinct classes:

### Class A: High-Frequency Functional Roots
These 15-20 roots make up the "skeleton" of the text. They are abstract and structural.
- **`ar`**: "To/For" or "Ground/Earth"
- **`al`**: "Near" or "Related to"
- **`ee`**: "Do/Make" (Generic Verb)
- **`aiin`**: "One" or "Element"

### Class B: Low-Frequency Content Nouns (Semantic Clusters)
Using co-occurrence clustering (Task 168), we identified semantic categories for rare words:
- **[Plant Name]**: Words appearing in label positions or "recipe" lists (e.g., `opan`, `fary`).
- **[Ingredient]**: Words co-occurring with "mix", "drink", "take".
- **[Star/Sky]**: Words appearing in the Cosmological sections.

## 3. The Topics (Semantic Map)
The text is not a narrative but a **technical manual**. The structure is repetitive because it describes procedures or lists properties.

- **Herbal Section**: Lists properties of plants. Sentences often start with the Plant Name (Topic) followed by attributes.
- **Recipes**: Imperative verbs (`qok-`) followed by list of ingredients (`o-` words).
- **Cosmology**: Descriptive states of stars/elements (`daiin` usage is higher).

## Conclusion
The "Grammar-First" approach reveals that the manuscript is highly structured. We can now "read" the syntax (Subject -> Verb -> Object) even if the specific nouns remain encrypted. The clustering adds a layer of semantic context, allowing us to distinguish "Ingredients" from "Stars" even without knowing their exact English names.
