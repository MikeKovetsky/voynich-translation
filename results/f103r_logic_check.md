*   **Line 4:** `ychedy` -> "AND herb/plant". This starts the line. The previous line ends with `chym!`. This suggests a continuation of a list or a new step starting with "And...".

## 3. Key Findings

1.  **"Take Water":** Line 10 (`daiin ol oain`) is a very strong match for "Take water" or "Take from the spring". This validates `daiin` as the recipe starter.
2.  **Lists:** Line 11 contains a clear list of ingredients (Fig, Herb, Geranium) separated by `shey` (measure/portion) or `chey`.
3.  **"And" (`y-`):** The usage is consistent with a conjunction. `yshdaiin` in Line 3 is particularly interesting. If `dain` is "Take", `sh-` might be a modifier, and `y-` is "And". "And [sh]-Take"? Or "And [she]-dain" (And the take)?
    *   *Hypothesis:* `sh-` is often "the" or "she" (pronoun).
    *   `yshdaiin` could be "And taking" or "And take".

## 4. Conclusion

The translation supports the "And" hypothesis. The recipe structure `daiin` -> Ingredients -> Process is visible, particularly in Lines 10-12.
