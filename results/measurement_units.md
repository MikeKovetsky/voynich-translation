## Contextual Evidence

### `saiin` (Cup)
- **Liquid Context**: `saiin ol aiin` ("Cup of Water") [f112r].
- **Dry Context**: `saiin ol che` ("Cup of Herb") [f112r].
- **Structure**: Consistently appears in `[Unit] ol [Substance]` pattern.

### `daiin` (Cup/Take)
- **Liquid Context**: `daiin ol aiin` ("Cup of Water") [f103r, f107v].
- **Mixed Context**: `daiin ol aiin chol` ("Cup of Water [and] Leaf") [f107v].
- **Note**: Higher frequency suggests it might be the default instruction ("Take") or a common vessel.

### `ar` (Handful)
- **Context**: Often precedes generic plant markers (`che`, `ch`), fitting the "Handful of Herbs" pattern common in recipes.

## Other Candidates
- **`qok`**: Likely an imperative verb ("Take", "Mix") rather than a unit, due to its position at the start of lines/phrases and high collocation with `aiin`.
